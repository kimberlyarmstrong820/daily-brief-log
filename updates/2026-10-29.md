4. **Android app flaw could steal Microsoft work tokens** — Microsoft patched an Authenticator for Android flaw that let a permissionless app impersonate Teams and grab work account tokens; exploitation needs user interaction and hasn't been seen in the wild. ([The Hacker News, Oct 27](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/microsoft-authenticator-flaw-let.html))

   <details>
   <summary>Deep dive</summary>

   Microsoft fixed CVE-2026-80097 (CVSS 8.6, Important) in Authenticator for Android. On Android, Authenticator also acts as a token broker for apps like Teams and Outlook. It exposes an activity, `AccountChooserActivity`, that any app on the device can launch, and it built token requests using whatever app name the caller claimed rather than checking who actually made the request. A malicious app claiming to be Teams could obtain Microsoft Graph tokens with read/write access to mail, files, and calendar.

   The researcher (Ofek Levin, via Enclave) reported the proof of concept needed no user interaction beyond a signed-in account on the device; Microsoft's advisory instead says exploitation requires installing a malicious app and the victim completing a sign-in step in Authenticator — the two accounts disagree on this point. Not exploited in the wild, not in CISA's KEV catalog as of Oct 26.

   **Fix:** update Authenticator for Android to v6.2606.4246+ (enforce via MDM where devices are managed). Microsoft's advisory does not say whether updating invalidates tokens a malicious app may have already obtained before the patch.

   </details>
